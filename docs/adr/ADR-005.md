# ADR-005 — Engineering Context

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-09-09
- **Decision:** YH will use an `EngineeringContext` as a contextualized, read-only representation of relevant engineering knowledge selected for a specific execution. It is produced on the Engineering Core/Application side and passed to the Runtime through `ExecutionRequest`. It does not own engineering knowledge, replace Specifications, or expose retrieval mechanisms such as RAG.

## Context

ADR-003 established that YH Specifications contain structured engineering knowledge through Requirements and Scenarios.

ADR-004 established the Runtime Boundary and the `ExecutionRequest` contract. The runtime needs enough engineering information to execute a WorkItem correctly, but it must not receive YH domain aggregates directly or become responsible for engineering knowledge.

A future YH implementation will also require mechanisms such as RAG to retrieve relevant project knowledge, previous decisions, specifications, and other information. Retrieval is a mechanism for finding context; it is not itself the engineering context or its source of authority.

Therefore YH needs an explicit concept between authoritative engineering knowledge and runtime execution:

```text
Engineering Knowledge
        |
        v
Context Selection / Policy
        |
        v
EngineeringContext
        |
        v
ExecutionRequest
        |
        v
Runtime
```

## Decision

YH will introduce `EngineeringContext` as a contextualized representation of engineering knowledge relevant to a particular execution.

Conceptually:

```text
EngineeringContext
├── knowledge
├── requirements
└── engineeringConstraints
```

The exact TypeScript representation is intentionally left for a later implementation decision.

## 1. EngineeringContext belongs to the Engineering side

`EngineeringContext` is produced from Engineering Core knowledge and Application-level context selection.

It is not a Runtime-owned concept.

The Runtime consumes the context but does not determine which engineering information is authoritative or relevant according to YH governance.

```text
Engineering Core / Application
          |
          v
EngineeringContext
          |
          v
Runtime Boundary
```

## 2. EngineeringContext is contextualized, not authoritative

The authoritative source remains the underlying Engineering Core knowledge.

For example:

```text
Specification
    |
    v
Requirement + Scenario
    |
    v
EngineeringContext
```

`EngineeringContext` is therefore a projection or contextualization for execution, not a second source of truth.

Changes to the context do not modify the Specification, Intent, WorkItem, or other engineering state.

## 3. Requirements retain their semantic structure

Requirements must not be flattened into arbitrary strings when included in the context.

Following ADR-003 and the OpenSpec-aligned semantics, relevant requirements preserve at least:

```text
Requirement
├── identity / name
├── normative statement
└── scenarios
      ├── condition / WHEN
      └── expected behavior / THEN
```

This allows the runtime/agent to reason about required behavior rather than receiving only an unstructured summary.

## 4. EngineeringContext contains WHAT, not HOW

EngineeringContext may provide the behavioral requirements and relevant engineering knowledge needed to execute a task.

It must not become a generic container for every engineering artifact.

In particular, the following remain conceptually separate:

```text
WHAT
Specification
└── Requirement
    └── Scenario

HOW
Design / technical decisions

IMPLEMENTATION
WorkItem / implementation tasks
```

Relevant design or implementation information may be included as supporting knowledge when required by the execution, but this does not make Design or Tasks intrinsic structural categories of `EngineeringContext`.

## 5. Engineering constraints are distinct from execution constraints

Engineering constraints describe limits imposed by the engineering context or project.

Examples may include architectural constraints, compatibility requirements, or project rules.

Execution constraints belong to `ExecutionRequest` and describe operating conditions for the runtime.

Therefore:

```text
EngineeringContext
└── engineeringConstraints

ExecutionRequest
└── executionConstraints
```

The distinction prevents runtime operating instructions from becoming engineering knowledge.

## 6. Instructions are not an intrinsic EngineeringContext category

`EngineeringContext` must not become a prompt container.

Runtime-specific instructions, operational directives, or prompt formatting belong to the execution boundary/runtime side.

Conceptually:

```text
EngineeringContext
    = engineering knowledge relevant to execution

ExecutionRequest
    = objective + workspace + engineering context + execution constraints
```

This keeps EngineeringContext independent from a particular model, prompt format, or runtime.

## 7. RAG is a retrieval mechanism, not part of the context model

YH may later use RAG to retrieve relevant engineering knowledge.

The architecture should therefore allow:

```text
Engineering Memory / Knowledge Sources
              |
              v
        Retrieval / RAG
              |
              v
      Context Selection Policy
              |
              v
      EngineeringContext
```

However, `EngineeringContext` must not contain concepts such as:

```text
embedding
vectorStore
retrievalQuery
chunk
RAGProvider
```

Those belong to the retrieval implementation.

RAG determines how information is found; YH determines what engineering information is authoritative and what may be supplied for an execution.

## 8. Authority must remain explicit

Not all information retrieved for an execution has the same authority.

The context model must allow YH to distinguish authoritative engineering requirements from supporting information when this distinction is relevant.

Conceptually:

```text
EngineeringContext
├── authoritative requirements
├── supporting knowledge
└── engineering constraints
```

The exact metadata used to represent authority is not fixed by this ADR.

The important architectural decision is that authority is determined by YH, not by the runtime or retrieval mechanism.

## 9. EngineeringContext is execution-specific

A project may contain a large amount of engineering knowledge, but an execution should receive only the relevant subset.

For example:

```text
Project Knowledge
       |
       +-- Specification A
       +-- Specification B
       +-- Decision C
       +-- Previous Work D
       +-- Project Rules
              |
              v
       Context Selection
              |
              v
       EngineeringContext for WorkItem X
```

Therefore EngineeringContext is not intended to be a permanent copy of all project knowledge.

## 10. EngineeringContext is not a Domain aggregate

This ADR does not require `EngineeringContext` to become a Domain aggregate.

It is better understood as an Application/integration representation created for execution.

The domain continues to own authoritative concepts such as:

- Intent
- WorkItem
- Specification
- Requirement
- Scenario
- Review
- Release

The Application layer can contextualize those concepts into the neutral representation required by the Runtime Boundary.

## 11. EngineeringContext crosses the Runtime Boundary

The resulting execution flow is:

```text
Intent
   |
   v
Specification
   |
   v
Requirement / Scenario
   |
   v
Context Selection
   |
   v
EngineeringContext
   |
   v
ExecutionRequest
   |
   v
Runtime Boundary
   |
   v
Pi / Native Runtime
```

The runtime receives the contextualized engineering information without receiving ownership of the underlying engineering model.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Runtime execution receives structured engineering context without coupling to YH domain aggregates.
- Requirements and Scenarios retain their semantic meaning.
- Specifications remain the source of truth.
- RAG can be introduced later without coupling the Runtime Boundary to vector stores or retrieval technology.
- Engineering constraints remain distinct from runtime operating constraints.
- Runtime prompts and instructions remain separate from engineering knowledge.
- The model supports execution-specific context rather than sending the entire project to an agent.
- Authority remains under YH governance.

### Negative

- Context selection becomes an explicit Application responsibility.
- A contextualization/mapping step is required between domain knowledge and runtime input.
- Supporting knowledge may require provenance/authority metadata as the system evolves.
- A future RAG implementation will need clear policies for relevance and authority.

## What this ADR does not decide

This ADR deliberately does not decide:

- the final TypeScript structure of `EngineeringContext`;
- the persistence model for engineering knowledge;
- the RAG technology or vector database;
- embedding models;
- retrieval algorithms;
- context window/token budgeting;
- prompt construction;
- the final `ExecutionRequest` TypeScript contract;
- guardrail implementation;
- Verification/Evidence design;
- the final OpenSpec adapter;
- runtime-specific context formats.

## Decision summary

YH will treat `EngineeringContext` as a **contextualized, execution-specific representation of relevant engineering knowledge**.

It is created on the Engineering Core/Application side, preserves structured Requirements and Scenarios, may include relevant supporting knowledge and engineering constraints, and crosses the Runtime Boundary through `ExecutionRequest`.

It does not own engineering knowledge, become a prompt, expose RAG internals, or transfer engineering authority to the runtime.
